# RigDesk Business Model Canvas

**Direct equipment booking, a ready workforce, and a traceable job-to-payment workflow.**

> RigDesk removes the broker, not the trust.

**Recommended launch:** an asset-light B2B marketplace in one Karnataka service cluster, initially focused on excavators/JCBs, tippers and borewell rigs. Build reliable supply and repeat contractor demand before expanding geography. All prices below are proposals to test, not existing revenue or partner commitments.

## The Nine Building Blocks

| Block | RigDesk business design |
| --- | --- |
| **Customer segments** | Paying demand: small/mid-sized contractors, developers, farms and infrastructure subcontractors. Supply: independent machine owners and regional fleets. Adjacent participants: skilled operators, drillers, mechanics and contracted fuel stations. Start with repeat buyers rather than every equipment category. |
| **Value propositions** | Customers compare suitable equipment, rates, owner capacity and bids, then track the booking in one place. Owners reduce idle time and reach relevant work opportunities. Multi-owner quantity allocation supports bulk jobs. Worker hiring fills operator gaps. Inspection and signed booking records improve traceability; approved fuel vouchers can lower operating costs. Faster settlement is an objective dependent on verified completion, disputes and a functioning payment partner. |
| **Channels** | Local fleet/site onboarding, contractor associations, equipment workshops and dealer referrals; search pages and opt-in WhatsApp outreach; the web application and regional-language demos. Acquisition should be geographic and job-led, not broad paid advertising before local supply is dependable. |
| **Customer relationships** | Self-service comparison and repeat booking, assisted first-job onboarding, a named coordinator for bulk work, transparent cancellation terms and staffed dispute support. Build loyalty through useful job records, operator availability and clear settlement updates rather than restrictive contact hiding alone. |
| **Revenue streams** | Primary: a disclosed equipment-booking commission, starting with a **5% pricing experiment**. Later: fleet software subscriptions, separately quoted bulk-project coordination, employer-paid worker placement and contracted fuel referral fees. Promoted listings must be labelled. Financing referral revenue only through an approved lender arrangement; never count loan principal, worker wages or voucher discounts as platform revenue. |
| **Key resources** | Reliable owner inventory, real availability and capacity data, repeat contractor relationships, booking/inspection records, trained support and review staff, the software/database, private document storage and payment-provider relationships. Trust comes from reviewed evidence and operating performance, not merely a badge. |
| **Key activities** | Recruit/review owners and workers; match demand and capacity; process bookings/bids; resolve scheduling conflicts; verify completion; manage disputes and partner settlement; operate vouchers; secure personal documents; measure service quality and repeat usage. |
| **Key partners** | Equipment owners and fleets; contractor networks; workshops and operator-training centres; approved fuel stations and attendant teams; payment/settlement providers; licensed financing/insurance partners where appropriate; mapping, hosting and communications providers. These are target partnerships, not claims that agreements already exist. |
| **Cost structure** | Product engineering, cloud/maps/communications, field onboarding, identity/licence review, payment processing, job support, fraud/dispute handling, sales and compliance. Track variable cost per completed job separately from fixed operating costs. Pump discounts need an explicit funding/reimbursement agreement; they are not free margin. |

## Revenue Sequence

1. **Prove the core:** free owner listing and a clearly disclosed completion-based booking fee. Do not surprise both sides with duplicate charges.
2. **Monetise repeat use:** test fleet tools at **INR 999/month** only when owners find recurring operational value. Keep basic listing access available.
3. **Add managed projects:** quote a coordination fee for complex multi-machine jobs and distinguish it from the booking commission.
4. **Expand carefully:** test employer-paid worker placement, fuel referrals and financing referrals only when actual operations and partner agreements support them. Do not charge workers upfront simply to appear in search.

These experiments require customer interviews and willingness-to-pay tests. The UI's existing estimate fee and the new payment layer's commission policy must be reconciled before charging real customers; these proposals do not silently change either configuration.

## Illustrative Unit Economics

| Item | Example hypothesis |
| --- | ---: |
| Completed equipment work value | INR 100,000 |
| Platform fee at 5% | INR 5,000 |
| Payment-processing allocation | INR 1,000 |
| Variable support/verification | INR 700 |
| Acquisition/referral allocation | INR 800 |
| Risk/refund operating provision | INR 500 |
| Contribution before fixed costs and tax | **INR 2,000** |

This is a sensitivity example, not a forecast. Provider charges, refunds, taxation and transaction structure must be validated. GMV is not revenue. A vendor-funded 5% fee would leave INR 95,000 before other agreed deductions; a customer-funded fee changes the customer's payable total instead. Choose and disclose one charging model.

**Illustrative break-even:** at INR 200,000 monthly fixed costs and INR 2,000 contribution per completed job, 100 completed jobs/month cover fixed costs. If contribution falls to INR 1,000, the requirement doubles. Neither volume nor costs are established.

## Pilot and Evidence

Run a controlled 90-day pilot with a target of 20-30 reviewed owners, 40-60 genuinely available machines and 10-15 repeat contractor accounts. These are recruitment targets, not traction claims.

Measure request-to-confirmation time, fill rate, equipment utilisation, cancellations, repeat bookings, disputes, acquisition cost, contribution/job and actual sign-off-to-payout time. Compare like-for-like quotations before claiming customer savings. Review the results before adding another service cluster.

The strongest defensible advantage is local supply density plus reliable execution and transaction history. A QR code, map or AI recommendation alone is easy to copy. Keep identity documents private; do not sell personal document data. Avoid promising guaranteed availability, instant payment or legal authenticity beyond the evidence the system can establish.

## What Exists Versus What Needs Operations

The code includes equipment bookings, auctions, quantity allocation, QR-backed booking records, worker profiles/hiring, fuel vouchers and a disabled payment/inspection service. Live functionality requires the corresponding Supabase migrations and deployment. Real identity review, approved fuel discounts, staffed disputes, payment onboarding, financing, payroll and automated recorded calls need additional operational/provider setup.

**Pitch:** RigDesk is the transaction workflow for local heavy-equipment work: find the machine, compare the offer, secure the operator, track the job and settle against verified completion. Equipment owners gain work opportunities; contractors gain clearer choice and accountable execution.
